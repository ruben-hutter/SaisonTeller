# shallot (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:**
- Reads as an **onion** or **beetroot/radish**, possibly even a garlic bulb. Problems: the bulb is a smooth, symmetrical teardrop shape; the color is a flat dusty mauve/pink (reads as onion skin, not shallot's coppery-magenta); the sprout on top looks like a stem with a random stick poking out the side; the three root hairs at the bottom are too long and make it look like a turnip.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Show the bulb cluster (oblong, not round):** Shallots are elongated and often drawn as 2–3 cloves side-by-side with visible dividing lines. The single fat sphere is the #1 misread cause.
- **Fix the color:** Use warm coppery-red/rose-brown tones (e.g., #B05A5A–#C87F6F gradient or banding), not flat purple-mauve.
- **Clean up appendages:** One short curved sprout at the top; remove the weird side stick; shorten or remove the root hairs — they add clutter without meaning.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:**
- **Front view of an oblong bulb, slightly pointed at top, with 1–2 vertical dividing lines showing cloves, a coppery-magenta color, one short sprout, and a small tuft of roots** — the classic shallot/garlic-family silhouette but longer and slimmer than an onion, with warm red skin.
