# mint (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:** A bug — specifically a moth, butterfly, or beetle: symmetrical dark-green "wings" flanking a pale oval "body," with antennae-like strokes on top, plus a stem "tail." Possibly also reads as a tulip or lotus flower. Nothing about it says mint.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Kill the symmetry + center oval.** Two wing-like leaves flanking a pale central shape is the textbook bug/flower formula. Make leaves unsymmetrically clustered or stacked.
- **Add serrated, toothed leaf edges and pointed tips.** Mint leaves are ovate with serrated margins — this is the single most distinguishing feature. Current leaves are smooth blobs with notches that read as moth wings.
- **Show 2–3 leaves on a visible stem with opposite branching**, not leaves floating around a central bud. Deepen the green (current pale green reads as succulent/aloe or tulip stem).

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:** A vertical sprig: a stem with 2–3 pairs of opposite, serrated, pointed ovate leaves (slightly angled upward), maybe one leaf at the tip — drawn in a single saturated medium green with darker vein lines. Think "herb sprig" (like the parsley/basil icon archetype) rather than "blossom."
