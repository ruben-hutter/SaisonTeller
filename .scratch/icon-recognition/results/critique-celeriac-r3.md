# celeriac (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW**
- Looks like an avocado or green apple with sprouts/leaves on top — a generic "sprouting fruit" icon.
- The hollow white center reads as a slice/bite, not a root interior.
- Beige tendril "roots" at bottom are too few and hair-like; read as spider legs or drips.
- The side stripe reads as a bite mark or peel strip, not fibrous root skin.
- Overall: too smooth and perfectly round. Celeriac's defining trait — gnarly, bumpy, irregular root ball — is completely absent.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES**
- **Kill the sprout stems.** Five floppy leaf stems read as decorative foliage. Celeriac should have short, thick stalks cut off at the top (stubby celery stubs), or just 1–2 small leaves — not a plant growing out of a ball.
- **Make the silhouette lumpy.** Add irregular bumps, a rough outline, and a tapered root end (small taproot) at the bottom instead of a clean sphere. Asymmetry = celeriac.
- **Fix the interior.** Either remove the white cutout (whole root, solid) or make a proper cut-half view: creamy white/off-white flesh with a thin green-tan rim. The current white void looks like avocado flesh gone wrong.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW**
Whole knobby root ball shown straight-on or slightly 3/4: bumpy tan-green/green sphere, tapering to a stubby taproot at bottom, with a few short cut celery stalk stubs (and optionally 1–2 small leaves) on top. If cut open, show the cross-section: white flesh, thin skin, faint ring pattern — that ring pattern is the single most identity-giving detail.
