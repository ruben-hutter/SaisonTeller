# lovage (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:**
- Reads as a **green pepper/bell pepper** or a generic **tulip/flower bud**. The two large rounded lobes + central pointed shape = classic bell-pepper silhouette.
- The thin stem under a rounded mass also suggests a **radish/turnip** in some readings.
- Lovage's actual identity — tall stalks with many serrated, compound celery-like leaves — is completely absent. Nothing here says "herb."

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Kill the lobes.** Replace the two fat rounded side-shapes with a cluster of small, individual leaflets (3–7) on visible stems, fanning from one node. Lovage is a compound leaf, not a solid mass.
- **Add serration and celery-like ribbing.** Serrated/toothed leaf edges and a slightly ridged stalk are the #1 visual cue separating lovage (maggi plant) from generic leaves.
- **Emphasize the stalk.** Lovage is stalk-dominant (it's eaten like celery). Make the stem long, straight, and proportionally prominent — one leaf cluster on top of a tall ribbed stalk, not a fat bud on a stick.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:**
**Straight-on, upright:** a tall light-green ribbed celery-like stalk with one umbrella-shaped cluster of dark-green serrated leaflets fanning at the top — like a stylized flat-leaf parsley/celery bunch, clearly a *herb*, not a fruit or flower.
