# kale (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:** A potted plant / succulent / aloe, or abstract leaves on a stick. Causes: central vertical stem with symmetric rounded lobes, plus a small trapezoid "pot" base at the bottom. No curly/frilly edges anywhere — that's kale's defining trait, and it's completely missing. The horizontal dashes read as stitches or seams, not leaf texture.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Add ruffled/frilled leaf margins** — wavy, bumpy scalloped edges on every leaf. This is non-negotiable; smooth oval leaves = generic plant.
- **Delete the stem and pot base.** Kale is sold as a bundle/head of leaves. Replace with 3–5 broad leaves fanning up from a cut stem base, or tied bundle silhouette.
- **Make leaves dominant and overlapping**, not a background pair behind a stick. Use dark green + one lighter leaf for depth; bump leaf size to fill ~80% of the canvas.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:** Front view of a **bundle of 3–5 upright ruffled leaves** (kale bunch as sold in grocery stores), each leaf with highly wavy/crinkled edges, tightly grouped at the base. Deep green palette, one accent light-green leaf. Think "mini palm fronds with lettuce-curl edges" — that silhouette is instantly kale.
