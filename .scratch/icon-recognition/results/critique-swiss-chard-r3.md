# swiss-chard (round 3)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:**
- Reads as a **cactus** (prickly pear/saguaro) — tall green lobes with orange/yellow spines dotting them.
- The orange marks sit *on top of* the leaves like cactus spines/thorns.
- The upright lobed shapes with rounded tips mimic cactus pads/arms; the dark teal stripe reads like a cactus column.
- Chard's defining trait — **thick colored stalks (white/pink/red) with broad dark leaves** — is absent or unreadable.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Recolor the stems, not the leaves:** make thick, wide stalks in chard's signature colors (white, magenta, or red) running the full height, with dark green leaves attached at the top. Stalks = the identity cue.
- **Move the accent color off the leaf surface:** the orange/pink should be the *stem color*, not small marks floating on foliage. Delete the spine-like orange ovals entirely.
- **Change the silhouette:** replace lobed cactus-pad shapes with broad, wavy-edged leaves (oval/teardrop with crinkled or ruffled edges) fanning out from clustered stalk bases.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:**
- **Front view, fanned bunch:** 3–5 chard leaves standing upright like a bouquet — thick white or magenta stalks visible as wide center ribs, broad dark-green crinkly leaves attached at the top, stalks converging at the base. High contrast between stalk color and leaf color is the whole trick.
