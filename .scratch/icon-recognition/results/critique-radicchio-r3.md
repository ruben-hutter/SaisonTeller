# radicchio (round 3)

**1. What it reads as now**
- Reads as a **beet / red onion / ram’s skull**. The symmetrical dark-red mass with side “horns” and dangling cream strips suggests a beetroot with roots, or an abstract mask/face.
- The side leaves droop downward and detach visually — they read as ears, tusks, or drips, not cabbage leaves.
- Cream vein-like strokes are too sparse and squiggly, reading as cracks or whiskers, not leaf ribs.

**2. Top 3 fixes**
- **Round out the silhouette:** radicchio is a tight, layered ball. Replace the drooping side pieces with a plump rounded/oval body; the outer silhouette should be nearly circular with slight leaf-tip points on top.
- **Show overlapping leaf layers:** add 2–3 visible outer leaves wrapping around a paler inner heart (lighter red/magenta center). Layering is the #1 cue that separates cabbage-family produce from beets/onions.
- **Fix the veins:** thicker, curved cream ribs that follow each leaf’s curve, radiating from the base — like red cabbage veins. Remove the floating cream blob at the bottom (reads as a root or tooth).

**3. Canonical view**
- **Top-down / three-quarter view of a whole round head:** near-circular outline, cupped overlapping outer leaves, pale pink-white center heart, bold cream veins curving along each leaf. Essentially a red cabbage crossed with an artichoke silhouette — round, layered, veined.
