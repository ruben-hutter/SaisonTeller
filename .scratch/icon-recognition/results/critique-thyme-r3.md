# thyme (round 3)

1. **WHAT IT READS AS NOW:** A bare brown twig/branch with scattered random green berries or peas — reads as "peas on a vine," "berries on a stick," or just a diagonal line with dots. Causes: leaves are perfect detached ellipses (no leaf shape, no attachment to stem), stem is a plain uniform brown stick, tiny purple flowers at the tip look like decorative sparkles.

2. **TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Attach leaves directly to the stem** — small paired, opposite, oval leaves hugging the twig, so nothing floats.
- **Thin green stem, not thick brown stick** — thyme has a wiry green/woody stem; brown reads as dead branch/cinnamon.
- **Tiny leaves, many of them, dense along one stem** — the "small paired leaves on a skinny sprig" silhouette is the thyme signature; add a second smaller stem if needed.

3. **CANONICAL VIEW:** A single diagonal sprig with a thin green stem, dozens of tiny oval green leaves in opposite pairs along its full length, optionally 2–3 tiny purple/pink flower dots only at the very tip. Dense micro-leaves + green stem = unmistakable thyme.
