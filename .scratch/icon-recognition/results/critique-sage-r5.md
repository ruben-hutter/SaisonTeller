# sage (round 5)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW**
- It reads as a generic **plant sprig / tree branch / willow twig** — possibly a young bamboo or spring shoot.
- Why: smooth oval leaves evenly spaced along a wavy stem, no sage-specific traits. Sage's defining features — elongated lance-shaped leaves, fuzzy/velvety texture, gray-green dusty color, oppositely paired leaves — are all absent or wrong. The pale split-stripe leaves make them look like generic cartoon leaves or seed pods.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES**
- **Change leaf shape:** replace the round/oval leaves with long, narrow, slightly pointed lanceolate leaves that taper to a tip — sage leaves are oblong, not egg-shaped.
- **Opposite pairing & upward leaves:** put leaves in strict opposite pairs along the stem, angled upward along it, and end the stem with a terminal leaf cluster. This kills the "scattered willow branch" read instantly.
- **Color shift:** swap bright green for sage's dusty **gray-green / muted sage green** (#9CAE8E-ish stem, grayer leaves). Optional: one vein line or a subtle stippled/fuzzy edge for the velvety texture. Remove the odd split-stripe leaf detail.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW**
- A single vertical stem, slightly curving, with **3–4 opposite pairs of elongated pointed gray-green leaves** angled upward, plus a small leaf or bud cluster at the tip — the classic herb-sprig silhouette (like rosemary/thyme icons, but with broader lance-shaped leaves and dusty green palette).
