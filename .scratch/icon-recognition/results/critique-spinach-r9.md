# spinach (round 9)

**1. WHAT IT READS AS NOW:**
- Three overlapping leaves reads as **basil / mint / generic herb bouquet**, not spinach.
- The pointed center leaf with serrated side leaves is basil iconography 101.
- The pale green stem "trident" at bottom looks like a decorative flourish or a claw — distracting and confusing.
- No curl, no crinkle, no soft ovate silhouette = no "spinach" signal.

**2. TOP 3 FIXES:**
- **Kill the three-leaf cluster.** Spinach icons are almost always a *single* leaf or a tight 2-leaf pair with visible pale stems.
- **Fix the leaf shape:** make it ovate/spade-shaped with a slightly wavy or crinkled edge (bumpy scallops), rounded tip or soft point — not sharp serrated basil lobes.
- **Show the thin pale stem** emerging from the leaf base — the long tender stem is spinach's signature. Use light green for the vein/stem against a saturated mid-green blade.

**3. CANONICAL VIEW:**
One large ovate leaf seen flat from above, slightly crinkled/wavy margin, with a pale central vein and a thin light-green stem extending below — optionally a second smaller leaf tucked behind at a slight angle. Dark-to-mid green fill, light sage accents. That's the universal "spinach leaf" read.
